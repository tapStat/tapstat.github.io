The Google Analytics app, and the best alternative for online stores

Updated 25 October 2026 · 7 min read

If you run an online store, you probably open Google Analytics on your phone more than anywhere else. And you probably close it again a little frustrated. Here is why, and what to look for in an alternative.

What the official app does well

Google's Analytics app is free, it's made by the people who make GA4, and it covers every property type: websites, apps, lead generation, publishers and stores. You can browse the standard reports, open realtime, and read the automated insights Google generates. If you're an analyst who wants the GA4 report library in your pocket, it does the job.

Where store owners get stuck

A store owner doesn't want a report library. They pick up their phone with a handful of questions: how much did we sell today, is that normal, and if not, why? The general-purpose app makes those questions harder than they need to be:

  • Comparisons that mislead. Comparing today with yesterday makes every Monday look like a disaster and every Sunday look like a win. Stores need same-weekday comparisons, and "today so far" compared with the same point in time last week.
  • No pace. At 10:00 a revenue total says nothing on its own. What matters is whether you are ahead of or behind a normal day at 10:00.
  • The why takes a laptop. Splitting a drop into traffic, conversion rate and order value, then by channel and device, usually means building an exploration in the browser.
  • Ad spend lives elsewhere. Meta Ads, TikTok and Pinterest spend sit in other dashboards, so return on ad spend means opening three apps.
  • Nothing on your home screen. No widget, no watch tile. You have to open the app to know anything.
  • Profit is invisible. GA4 knows revenue, not what you keep after product costs, fees, shipping and ads.

What a store-focused GA4 app should do

Whatever you choose, look for these:

  1. It opens on revenue. Revenue, orders, average order value, conversion rate and refunds, before anything else.
  2. It compares like with like. Same weekday last week by default, and the same weekday last year when you want to see seasonality.
  3. It explains changes. Traffic, conversion or order value, and the channel, device or product behind it.
  4. It knows your ad spend. At least the Google Ads link and GA4's cost data import for other platforms, with ROAS per channel.
  5. It comes to you. Alerts that understand your normal week, a morning summary and a widget.
  6. It is read-only. An analytics app should never need permission to change your GA4 setup.

When to stick with the official app

If you analyse a content site or a mobile app, or you need the full GA4 report library and explorations, Google's app and the GA4 website are the right tools. tapStat deliberately does less, so the things a store owner checks every day take three seconds.

Questions

Is there an alternative to the Google Analytics app?

Yes. Apps such as tapStat read the same GA4 data through Google's official API. tapStat is built for online stores and adds widgets, same-weekday comparisons, why it changed, ROAS, profit and alerts.

Is it safe to connect a third-party app to Google Analytics?

Choose an app that asks only for read-only access. tapStat uses Google's analytics.readonly permission, so it can read reports but can't change your settings or data.

Does tapStat replace the GA4 website?

No. For deep analysis, explorations and setup, use the GA4 website. tapStat covers what a store owner checks every day, on the phone.
